Each bar's width is the proposal's annual cost; its height is the cost per QALY gained. Funding from the left until the money runs out maximises health. The height at which the money runs out is the threshold that the budget implies.

What the game is about

The threshold is a shadow price

Nobody needs to announce a cost-effectiveness threshold for one to exist. With a fixed budget, the last proposal funded sets it: anything less cost-effective would displace more health than it adds. This is the supply-side view of the threshold, which Claxton and colleagues estimated for the NHS at about £13,000 per QALY. New Zealand's PHARMAC works this way, with a fixed budget and no published threshold.

The rule of rescue

The enzyme replacement therapy helps six identified children with a fatal disease. It costs far more per QALY than anything else on the list. Funding it is a coherent choice only if you believe identified, severe need deserves more than its QALYs. The game shows exactly how much health elsewhere that belief costs.

Severity weights

The toggle mimics NICE's severity modifier, which since 2022 multiplies QALYs by 1.2 or 1.7 for conditions with large proportional or absolute shortfalls in expected health. Weighting changes which proposals win. The money and the unweighted health gained stay the same.

All fourteen proposals, their costs and their QALY gains are invented for teaching; they are loosely shaped like real decisions but describe no actual product or submission. "Classroom HEPA filtration" echoes the CASCADE school clean-air project, with an assumed effect. Costs are annual, in NZ dollars; QALYs are annual gains with no discounting. The best portfolio is solved exactly (0/1 knapsack).
