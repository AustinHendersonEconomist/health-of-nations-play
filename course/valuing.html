What the exercise shows

Why "worse than death"?

About a third of the 243 states have negative UK values. The figure is an average, so it depends heavily on how negative answers are scaled. In the 1993 UK survey, a respondent who preferred death was asked how many of ten years in that state, followed by full health, would make death and life equal. Those answers were transformed onto a scale bounded at −1, which is why the worst state scores −0.594 rather than −∞.

Whose values?

Official tariffs come from the general public imagining states they mostly have not lived in. Patients living with a condition often rate it higher, partly because they adapt. Using public values is a deliberate choice: taxpayers fund the health system. It means the QALY measures what the public would trade, not necessarily what patients experience.

From answer to money

If a treatment moved someone from your state to full health for ten years, the QALY gain is ten times the gap between 1 and the state's value. Multiply by a threshold to see what a health system would pay: NICE now accepts £25,000–35,000 per QALY. Your own answer and the population's can imply very different prices.
