Two prices
of a life

New Zealand's transport agency and its Treasury put very different values on keeping people alive and well. This page converts one into the other and explains why the gap remains.

Road safety · NZTA, 2023
$12.5m

The value of a statistical life used to appraise New Zealand transport projects (NZ dollars).

Public spending · Treasury CBAx
$36k

The value of one quality-adjusted life-year (QALY), a year in full health, used in NZ Treasury cost–benefit analysis (central value, 2022 dollars).

$12,500,000 ÷ $36,000 =347healthy years per life saved

Nobody has 347 healthy years left to lose. If the two numbers measured the same thing, a road death would have to cost several lifetimes. So either the arithmetic is unfair, or the numbers measure different things. In fact both are true.

Step 1 · Compare like with like

Turn a statistical life into a price per QALY

A statistical life is a whole remaining lifetime, valued in advance by the people who face a small risk. Before comparing it with a QALY, spread it over the years actually at stake, adjust those years for health, and discount them in the same way.

Step 2 · Why a gap remains

Six reasons the numbers disagree

Once the arithmetic is fair, the road-safety value per QALY is still several times the health value. Some of the remaining gap reflects real differences between the questions being asked. The rest is measurement noise and history that a consistent government might want to remove.

A real difference

Whose money is it?

A VSL asks what people would pay from their own incomes for safety: a demand-side value. A health threshold asks what a fixed public budget gives up elsewhere when it funds one more treatment: a supply-side value. The second is usually far lower. Claxton and colleagues estimated that the NHS spent about £12,936 to produce one QALY at the margin, well below what people say a healthy year is worth to them.

A real difference

What is being bought

A VSL prices tiny risk reductions spread across millions of people: one in 100,000 fewer deaths for each of 100,000 drivers is one statistical life. Nobody knows in advance whose life it is. A QALY threshold prices health for known patients, often small gains for people who are already ill. People value preventing sudden, early, involuntary death more than adding months at the end of an illness.

A real difference

How the death happens

Stated-preference studies find that people pay more to avoid deaths that are sudden, violent, imposed by others or feared ("dread" risks) than deaths of the same age from illness. Transport and environmental deaths carry those features. Whether policy should honour that premium is a value judgement, not a technical fix.

Partly measurement

How it is measured

VSLs come from wage premiums for risky jobs or from surveys. Survey answers often show scope insensitivity: willingness to pay barely rises when the risk reduction doubles, which inflates the implied value of each life. NZ's value rose from $4.88m to $12.5m in 2023 largely because the 1991 survey was replaced with a new study.

Partly measurement

Age, health and life expectancy

A single VSL is applied whatever the victim's age. The QALY approach counts the years that remain, so it values saving a child over saving a 90-year-old. Converting at one age (above) hides this. Move the age slider and the implied price per QALY changes several-fold.

Partly institutional

Different agencies, different histories

Transport, environment and health agencies set their values separately, often decades apart, with different discount rates and price bases. Tengs and colleagues catalogued 500 US life-saving interventions. Costs per life-year saved ran from cost-saving to more than $10 billion, and the median differed greatly by sector: about $19,000 for medicine against $2.8 million for toxin control.

Sources and caveats

Where the numbers come from

NZTA VSL, NZ$12.5m. Waka Kotahi NZ Transport Agency, Monetised Benefits and Costs Manual v1.6 (April 2023), based on Denne, Kerr et al., NZTA Research Report 698 (2023). The previous value of NZ$4.88m indexed a 1991 survey.

NZ Treasury QALY, about NZ$36k. The central QALY value in the CBAx tool, as reported by secondary sources (2022 dollars). Earlier versions used about NZ$33–38k. Check the current CBAx spreadsheet before citing.

NICE thresholds. £20,000–30,000 per QALY from 1999, raised to £25,000–35,000 for technology appraisals from April 2026. Highly specialised technologies use £100,000–300,000.

UK Green Book. HM Treasury guidance values a QALY at about £70,000 (the price base varies by source; some cite £60,000).

Supply-side estimate. Claxton K, Martin S, Soares M, et al. Methods for the estimation of the NICE cost effectiveness threshold. Health Technology Assessment 2015;19(14).

US HHS VSL. Office of the Assistant Secretary for Planning and Evaluation, Standard Values for Regulatory Analysis (2025): US$13.6m, 2024 dollars.

Cost per life-year across sectors. Tengs TO, Adams ME, Pliskin JS, et al. Five-hundred life-saving interventions and their cost-effectiveness. Risk Analysis 1995;15(3):369–390.

Life expectancy and quality norms. Remaining life expectancy is interpolated from approximate NZ period life tables (life expectancy at birth about 82.5 years). Quality weights are illustrative age norms, not a NZ value set. Exchange rates are approximate (NZ$1.70/US$, NZ$1.90/£).

What this page is not. It is a teaching tool, not guidance. VSLs and QALY thresholds answer different questions, and agencies may defensibly use different values. The point is to make the differences visible.
